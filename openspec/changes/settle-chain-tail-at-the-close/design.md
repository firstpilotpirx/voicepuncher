## Context

The service reports a chain as soon as one recognizer decoded it: `decide` in `command-spotting/src/streaming/decide/mod.rs` judges only the *fresh* words of a recognizer's text, those starting at or after the end of the utterance's last report (`Utterance::reported_until`, shared by both recognizers), and the chain matcher (`PhraseMatcher::anywhere`) needs the chain's words together. The English recognizer emits a word only once more audio came after it, so the last command of a chain shows up a decode or more after the ones before it. Traced on the user's recording session-4/5 ("that is all stop enter start", bench in real time, `RUST_LOG=…session=info` with a temporary print of each decode's words): the Russian recognizer reported "stop" (its stamp 1.72–2.10 s); the English one then had "stop 2.24–2.34, enter 2.88–3.06" fresh and reported "stop dictation, press enter" (its "stop" doubled: the peer `dedupe-close-reports-across-recognizers`); the Russian one decoded "start 2.76–3.18" and the English one "start 3.20–3.24" after that, but with the report's end at 3.06 the Russian "start" was not fresh and the English one stood alone among the fresh words, where "start" while dictating is no command (it is accepted only after "stop" or "enter", `accepted_after`). Every later decode up to the close saw the same lone word: the tail was lost in 3 real-time runs of 3. Sessions 4/6 and 4/8 keep their tail because the Russian recognizer hears "энтерстарт" as one word and reports the chain whole; 4/7 keeps it because "enter" alone is a command while dictating.

## Goals / Non-Goals

- Goals: the last command of a chain decoded after the ones before it were reported is reported once, after them, from the first recognizer whose words say so; no report changes for chains decoded whole; measured before and after on the user's recorded chains.
- Non-goals: a command the two recognizers report doubled with different stamps (`dedupe-close-reports-across-recognizers`), the bench's `--fast` mode (`fast-bench-reproduces-app-timing`), the English recognizer's lag, a new E2E timing bound for chains.

## Decisions

**D1. The reported commands are found in the recognizer's text, and a chain's commands after them are the tail.** `decide` takes what the utterance reported so far (`Reported`: the end of the last report and the commands of every report in order, from `Reports::sent`) instead of the bare bound. First `after_reported`: `commands_said` (new in `matcher/placed/mod.rs`: every command form said in the words, in order, whatever the state accepts, by `command_at`) over *all* the recognizer's words, and the longest end of the reported commands said in order marks the boundary, the index after its last word; none when the text holds none of them. Then `chain_tail`: `PhraseMatcher::anywhere` over all the words, the commands whose first word comes before the boundary counted as reported; with some reported and some left, the tail is reported at once (`Placement::Anywhere`, as a chain decoded whole is reported today). Else the fresh words are those from the boundary on that also start at or after `reported.until`, and the rules run over them as before. The commands, never the stamps, tell what is reported: the English recognizer stamps a word 0.65–1.1 s later than the Russian one (peer note of 2026-11-05), so its "enter" of a chain the Russian one reported starts after the report's end and, judged by time, came out again as "press enter" (the first E2E run of D3, session-4/5: `stop, enter, enter, start`); the peer `dedupe-close-reports-across-recognizers` (v0.46.3) drops a report only when its command set repeats one, so the partial chain is this change's. And the text, not the matcher's chain, finds the reported commands: by the time the English recognizer decodes its "enter", the app has carried out "stop" and switched to standby, where "stop" is no command, so the chain "stop enter" no longer matches and the lone "enter" counted as said alone (the E2E trace of the second run; `should_report_nothing_of_the_english_enter_of_a_chain_reported_before_the_state_switched`). On session-4/5 the Russian "stop enter" goes out, the English text holds both, so its words up to "enter" are reported, and the English "start" is the chain's tail at its first decode, 20 ms after the speech end. A rule by time (the first word's start against the bound, the first version of this change) kept the tail but doubled "enter"; a rule by the span's end re-reported "stop dictation, press enter" whenever the English "stop" straddled the Russian report's end. The alternative, teaching the matcher the last reported command so a lone fresh word accepted after it counts, needs the report inside the matcher's state; kept out. `before_a_pause` and the close's `into_closed` call `decide` with nothing reported, so both steps stay silent there. `doubles` still catches the same command heard again within `DOUBLE_GAP_SECS`, as for any command. The per-recognizer repeat count of `Reports::goes_out` (the peer's `repeats`: the n-th finding of a command set by one recognizer goes out once the utterance sent it fewer than n times) is dropped with its `heard` list: it counted findings, and a recognizer whose text holds a reported command no longer finds it, so its count fell one short and its next finding of the same command (the second "enter" of `should_report_a_command_said_twice_with_a_pause_between_twice`) stayed in, while the dropped finding still became `last` and doubled the other recognizer's; the text boundary covers what the count did, as the peer's two session tests show, and `Reports` keeps `last` and `sent` only.

**D2. The session test scripts the late tail.** In `command-spotting/src/streaming/session/tests/`, the English `Scripted` recognizer says "stop" 0.6 s, "enter" 0.9 s and "start" 1.2 s into the utterance with its usual lag, the Russian one hears "стоп" and "энтер" only, the audio is fed in 20 ms frames so the decodes see "stop enter" before "start" (as `should_report_a_command_one_recognizer_heard_while_the_other_still_decodes` feeds it); expected: the reports `["stop dictation", "press enter"]` then `["start dictation"]`, nothing else. A `decide` unit test holds the session-4/5 words and stamps from the trace with "stop dictation, press enter" reported up to 3.06 s and expects "start dictation" starting at 3.20 s; a second one with the whole chain reported expects nothing; a third one holds the English words up to "enter" with the chain "stop enter" reported by the Russian recognizer up to 2.5 s, before the English "enter" starts, and expects nothing; a fourth holds the E2E trace's words with the standby matcher the app switched to and expects nothing. All are red before D1.

**D3. The user's recorded chains in E2E.** A `@smoke @voice_samples` scenario in `e2e-test/tests/features/voice_control.feature` under the rule "A chain of commands is carried out in order" plays each recorded chain whose last command is accepted only after the one before it — "start" or "clear" while dictating: session-2 "stop clear" and "cancel clear", session-4 "that is all stop enter start" twice and "never mind cancel enter start", session-5 "stop clear", by their labels in `forms.jsonl` — cut with the margins the recorded steps use, in real time through the app as `carried_out` does, while dictating, and expects every command of its label in order, each once (6 clips of 1–4 s: about 30 s of real time). The chains ending in "enter" ("that is all stop enter", "stop clear enter", "stop enter") keep their tail today, as "enter" alone is a command while dictating (session-4/7); they stay covered by the `voice_samples` tests and `just streaming-bench --recorded`.

**D4. Measured before and after.** Before (this branch, v0.46.2 code), `just streaming-bench --recorded ~/.voicepuncher/voice-samples` in real time: session 4 three times: 4/5 lost "start" 3 of 3 (run 3 also reported "stop" twice), 4/6, 4/7 and 4/8 kept their tail; every session once: 3 of the 9 labelled chains lost their tail — 4/5 "start", 2/42 "cancel clear" and 5/10 "stop clear" heard without "clear" ("clear all text" is accepted while dictating only after "stop" or "cancel", like "start") — and 48 of 53 clips right in all, the other wrong clips ("restart" heard with a "cancel" before it, a lone "stop", "clear", "cancel" or "start" heard in speech without one) outside this change. After D1: the same runs; the acceptance bar holds when no chain loses its tail and no other clip gets worse.

| run | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| session 4 ×3 real time, chain tails lost | 3 (all 4/5) | 0 |
| sessions 1–6 real time, chain tails lost / chains | 3 / 9 (2/42, 4/5, 5/10) | 0 / 9 |
| sessions 1–6 real time, clips right | 48 / 53 | 51 / 53 |

After (b7d19723, 2026-11-05): session 4 three times, 7 of 7 clips right each run, 4/5 "stop enter start" whole; every session once: the wrong clips left are the same 2 outside this change ("restart" heard with a "cancel" before it on 2/43 and 5/9) and the same 4 false alarms (a lone "stop", "clear", "cancel" or "start" heard in speech); the delays are unchanged (median 408 ms, p90 919 ms, before 405 / 915).

## Risks

- `dedupe-close-reports-across-recognizers` edits `Utterance::report` and the session tests at the same time, and targets 0.46.3 too: whichever lands second merges `main` at `report` (D1 touches `decide`, not `report`) and takes the next version (`kit version-check`).
- A chain said twice in one utterance with a pause the gate missed ("stop enter … stop enter") is matched as one chain of four by `anywhere` when the pause is short; the reported prefix leaves the second pair as the tail, reported as today.
